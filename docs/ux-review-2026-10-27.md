# Vashti UX Review

Date: 2026-10-27

Live follow-ups: 2026-10-28, 2026-11-03

Status: two implementation passes; current browser and physical-device acceptance pending

Baseline: `772608c6` plus the Android startup keep-rule fix

## Executive Summary

Vashti has useful capabilities, but too many of their controls receive equal
visual weight. The main opportunity is not removing capabilities. It is giving
each screen one clear purpose, then revealing controls when they are relevant.

The clearest example is image viewing. A photo is currently presented inside a
settings-style dialog, with a permanent title, close button, version navigation,
library navigation, editing actions, download, flow navigation, removal, and
comparison. The picture feels like one field in a form instead of the content
the user came to see.

The signed-in follow-up also found concrete correctness problems: the mobile
model menu overflows and shifts the header, embedding-only models are offered
for chat/custom-model creation, Back leaves the gallery instead of closing its
photo, and the closed mobile sidebar remains in keyboard navigation. Address
these alongside the relevant UX work, not as optional cosmetic polish.

Recommended order:

1. Replace the framed image dialogs with one immersive viewer and a proper
   zoom/pan/swipe interaction model.
2. Separate image prompt composition, reference management, and advanced
   settings. Keep the compact composer and conversational generation flow.
3. Establish a consistent action hierarchy, touch-target policy, and overlay
   behavior across the app.
4. Simplify navigation and settings organization without hiding essential
   actions or changing privacy permissions.
5. Validate the result on the Pixel 6 before calling the interaction polished.

Do not combine this with new inference features, a new component framework, or
a Kuno API redesign. The October 28 approval additionally requests calmer Green
styling and an optional Grayscale theme.

## Design Direction

### Preserve

- The Vashti identity and green accent. A calmer interface does not require a
  different brand or abandoning the existing themes.
- Conversational image generation, immutable retry recipes, and the distinction
  between conversation ancestry and ordered input images.
- Server/device storage choices, encrypted local persistence, and explicit
  model-access permissions. Cleaner UI must not silently broaden access.
- Numbered version arrows in addition to gestures, including on mobile.
- Full Markdown, thinking/tool details, message statistics, and the message tree.
  Make secondary information optional, not unavailable.
- Full-page Notes editing, recoverable trash/history, and return-to-chat links.
- Account image defaults that are separate from a thread's working settings.
- Control over backgrounds, portrait/landscape placement, and readability.

### Apply Consistently

- Content first; one visually dominant action per working area.
- Neutral surfaces and dividers for structure. Reserve bright accent fills for
  primary actions and deliberate selection, not every available command.
- Use a toolbar for commands, a sheet for a focused adjustment task, a menu for
  occasional actions, and a full viewport for inspecting media.
- Keep frequent controls directly reachable. Do not solve clutter by putting
  everything into an overflow menu.
- One vertical scroll owner for the main content. A deliberately opened sheet
  may have its own scroll area; the ordinary conversation must not gain nested
  scrolling to compensate for height bugs.
- Gestures supplement controls. Every important action remains accessible
  without knowing a gesture or using precise dragging.

## Fullscreen Image Viewer Proposal

This is the first recommended implementation. It follows the requested
photo-viewer direction rather than extending the existing dialog layout.

### Presentation

- Cover the app's usable viewport with a neutral dark viewing surface. Do not
  leave the sidebar, chat header, modal border, or framed card visible around it.
- Fit the image into that viewport without cropping or stretching. A landscape
  image on a portrait phone will still have letterboxing; fullscreen means the
  viewing surface is fullscreen, not that every image must fill every pixel.
- Put controls over the image/letterbox region. They must not participate in
  image layout or make the image shrink when they appear.
- Top overlay: Back, a compact position indicator, and More.
- Bottom overlay: Edit, Download, and Compare when an original is available.
  Comparison remains a dedicated hold control, away from image gestures.
- Put generation details, original flow, copy-to-gallery, and removal in More
  or a details sheet. Keep destructive actions separated and explicitly named.
- Use subtle local scrims behind controls, not another permanent dark filter
  over the entire image. The original should remain inspectable as generated.
- Respect Android system bars, gesture navigation, cutouts, and orientation.
  Do not change the whole app's edge-to-edge policy as an incidental viewer fix.

### Chrome Visibility

- Open with controls visible. A single tap on the image toggles the overlays.
- Fade controls after roughly three seconds of inactivity, with the timeout
  tuned on the phone. Tapping to reveal them restarts the timeout.
- Do not hide controls while a menu/sheet is open, Compare is held, a gesture is
  active, or keyboard focus is in the toolbar. Keyboard use keeps them visible.
- Hidden controls are not invisible focus targets or invisible click targets.
- A short tap must not also navigate, zoom, or dismiss the viewer.
- Escape/Back and an accessible close/back control remain available; the user
  must not be trapped because the overlays faded.

### Gesture Ownership

| State | Input | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fitted image | Single tap | Toggle overlays. |
| Fitted image | Horizontal drag | Drag the library carousel directly, then settle to a neighbor. |
| Fitted image | Short/mostly vertical movement | Do not accidentally navigate. |
| Any image | Pinch | Zoom around the fingers' midpoint, preserving the inspected detail. |
| Zoomed image | One-finger drag | Pan within the image, not through the library. |
| Any image | Double tap | Zoom into the tapped detail; double tap again returns to fit. |
| Zoomed image | Pinch back to fit | Return to the normal library-swiping state. |
| Image boundary | Drag farther | Small bounded resistance; no blank canvas or runaway navigation. |
| Compare control | Hold/release | Show first submitted reference / return to output. |
| Desktop | Wheel/trackpad and explicit zoom controls | Defined zoom/pan behavior without zooming the surrounding page. |

The first implementation should not add swipe-down-to-dismiss. It introduces
another gesture arbitration problem without being necessary for the primary
workflow. Back closes the top sheet/menu first, then the viewer, returning to
the exact gallery or flow position. Zoom alone should not require multiple Back
presses before leaving the photo.

Use a deliberate distance/velocity threshold and direct visual feedback. Do not
reuse a release-only left/right detector as if it were a complete carousel. A
new drag should be able to interrupt settling; users must not wait through a
decorative animation before navigating again. Do not default to very long
multi-photo flings.

### Library Versus Versions

- In Gallery, the main swipe sequence is the entire library, including crossing
  loaded-page boundaries. This already exists functionally and must be kept.
- A retry stack is secondary. Show one small version chip when applicable;
  opening it reveals the versions. Do not permanently display a second pair of
  arrow controls competing with library navigation.
- Retain explicit previous/next controls where needed, especially for keyboard
  and assistive use. Hide irrelevant pairs for a single-image collection.
- Browsing versions must not silently choose the active conversation branch.
  Keep branch selection explicit and separate from inspecting an image.
- The same viewer should serve gallery results, flow results, references, and
  chat attachments. Each caller provides its navigation collection and actions.
  Sharing the viewer must not merge storage scopes or change authorization.

### Loading and Responsiveness

- Open immediately from the thumbnail/decoded preview, with stable geometry.
  Replace it with the original without a blank intermediate frame.
- Decode the selected original and prefetch only a bounded neighborhood. Do not
  decode the entire image library into RAM.
- Keep UI-only state local to the viewer. Moving a finger must not rerender the
  whole app, parse prompts, or trigger network saves on every frame.
- Zoom uses the original image, not an enlarged thumbnail. Downloads remain the
  original bytes; no new lossy conversion or WebP requirement.
- Preserve gallery scroll position, density, loaded pages, and focus on close.
- A failed image offers a targeted retry; it should not collapse the viewport
  or force a full app refresh.
- Honor reduced motion. Test one-finger, multi-touch, keyboard, and screen-reader
  paths independently; desktop drag tests cannot establish pinch quality.

## Prioritized Backlog

P1: core-task friction or interaction/accessibility correctness to address first.
P2: repeated usability cost. P3: useful follow-up, not required for this pass.
Source paths below are relative to `apps/vashti/web/src/` unless stated otherwise.

### UX-01 / P1: Image Viewing Is Over-Framed

**Evidence:** browser portrait/landscape/desktop; `images/ImageGallery.tsx:307`,
`images/ImageFlow.tsx:1565`, `images/controls.tsx:4`, `images/images.css:84`.
The viewer shares a form-dialog structure and permanent action rows. Landscape
can require scrolling just to reach image actions.

**Change:** implement the fullscreen proposal above, with overlay controls that
do not resize the image. **Acceptance:** no scrolling the viewing surface to
find controls at portrait, landscape, or short desktop heights; tapping changes
chrome visibility without changing image bounds.

### UX-02 / P1: Zooming Needs a Real Image Interaction Model

**Evidence:** `images/SwipeSurface.tsx:3` tracks a single primary pointer and
steps after release; `images/images.css:132` allows browser pinch zoom but does
not implement image zoom/pan. The chat attachment viewer is separate and also
lacks an image transform model (`attachments.tsx:339`).

**Change:** one gesture owner with explicit fit/zoom/pan/swipe states.
**Acceptance:** pinch stays anchored, zoomed dragging cannot change photos,
double tap restores fit, cancelled gestures recover, and repeated swipes work
without waiting for previous settling. Validate on the Pixel, not only by mouse.

### UX-03 / P1: Make Back Behavior Consistent Across Overlays

**Evidence:** chat attachments push a viewer history entry
(`AppShell.tsx:741`); gallery and image-flow viewers use local dialog state.
In the live follow-up, browser Back from an open gallery photo returned to
`/app/images`, skipping the underlying gallery. This is now browser-reproduced;
it is not proof of a particular Android Back failure. Device behavior still
needs testing.

**Change:** define one overlay/back policy. **Acceptance:** Back/Escape closes
the top layer before leaving its page; returning from Edit or a referenced note
preserves the original context; closing a viewer restores the exact grid/flow
position. Test browser history and Android Back separately.

### UX-04 / P1: Modal Focus Behavior Is Inconsistent

**Evidence:** `common.tsx:33` renders an ARIA modal confirmation without initial
focus, focus containment, background inertness, or opener restoration. Image
panels use native `dialog.showModal()` and restore the opener instead.
In the live avatar editor, initial focus remained on the background trigger.
Separately, Shift+Tab from the main mobile hamburger moved focus into a history
row in the closed sidebar. `Sidebar.tsx:138` keeps that offscreen content mounted
without an inert/hidden policy. This is not just an ARIA labeling issue.

**Change:** standardize modal behavior around a small accessible shared
primitive; do not just add `aria-modal` to more divs. **Acceptance:** Tab cannot
reach the obscured app, Escape closes only the appropriate layer, cancelling
returns focus to the triggering control, and busy/destructive states remain
announced. Closed off-canvas navigation is also excluded from tab/screen-reader
navigation; opening it manages focus and closing restores the opener. Verify
with keyboard and TalkBack.

### UX-05 / P1: Important Touch Targets Are Too Small

**Evidence:** image toolbar buttons measured 40 x 40; version arrows are
30-pixel minimums (`styles.css:4889`); message actions are 34 x 32 minimums
(`styles.css:4928`); image expand control is 32 x 24 (`images/images.css:173`).

**Change:** use a project target of at least 44 x 44 CSS pixels for standalone
touch controls, with smaller glyphs inside. This is a design target, not a claim
of a completed accessibility conformance audit. **Acceptance:** hit regions do
not overlap, dense controls remain usable at 320-pixel width, and rows do not
become taller merely because a label or icon changes.

### UX-06 / P2: Separate Image Composition From Advanced Settings

**Evidence:** expanded mobile fixture exposes references, prompt, edit-source
mode, 17 aspect presets, dimensions, negative prompt, steps, CFG, seed, count,
attention, prompt improvement, and default-management commands in one dock.
The header settings button and expand affordance both lead to this state.

**Change:** retain the compact one-line composer. Tapping the prompt opens an
ordinary writing state; reference count opens reference management; sliders
opens a settings sheet. Keep Send/Stop reachable. **Acceptance:** choosing a
setting does not require navigating through the prompt, and reading a generated
image does not require leaving a huge settings dock open.

### UX-07 / P2: Clarify Image Result Actions

**Evidence:** flow results show Edit and Continue from this image together
(`images/ImageFlow.tsx:793`). `editResult` prepares an iterative edit and clears
its prompt; Continue selects the result as the leaf. They are distinct but the
icons do not explain the distinction on touch.

**Change:** make Edit the direct editing action and label branch navigation in
a contextual menu/sheet. Preserve both underlying operations. Group generation
recipe details under Info. **Acceptance:** a user can tell whether an action
prepares a new edit, changes the visible branch, or generates another seed before
triggering it; choosing a branch must not masquerade as editing pixels.

### UX-08 / P2: Reduce Reference-Strip Machinery

**Evidence:** each reference has its own number, image, two reorder controls,
remove, and import-settings action. Expanded mobile composition can show both
horizontal reference scrolling and vertical settings scrolling.

**Change:** preserve image numbering, tap-to-inspect, and accessible reorder
buttons. Open a focused reference sheet for ordering/removal; put uncommon
metadata import there. Drag reordering may supplement, not replace, the buttons.
**Acceptance:** adding/replacing/moving an image makes the submitted order
obvious; the ordinary composer does not grow with the number of references.

### UX-09 / P2: Gallery Loading Should Feel Like a Photo Grid

**Evidence:** fixed square tiles are a good existing improvement. The fixture
still shows repeated Loading image text, explicit Load more pagination, and
density +/- controls with the visual weight of primary commands
(`images/ImageGallery.tsx:230`).

**Change:** use quiet thumbnail placeholders, one aggregate loading/error
status, automatic near-end page loading with an explicit retry fallback, and a
small density control under View. Preserve fixed geometry and bound work.
**Acceptance:** delayed/offscreen thumbnails do not move the scroll anchor;
returning from a photo or changing density does not restart the library at top.

### UX-10 / P3: Add Lightweight Gallery Organization

**Evidence:** the gallery exposes density and stack counts but no date grouping,
search/filter, or multi-selection. It has timestamps and session relationships
in the data (`images/ImageGallery.tsx`).

**Change:** start with quiet date groupings and a filter for the current flow;
consider multi-select Download/Remove after the viewer is stable. Do not add a
permanent filter toolbar or editing buttons to every tile. **Acceptance:** a
large library is navigable without sacrificing its photo-first density.

### UX-11 / P2: Give Loading, Saving, and Failure Different Treatments

**Evidence:** multiple independent RetroLoader/text/error patterns exist; some
image operations use one shared error string. Image downloads have a busy state
but no uniform completion destination/status. The app's native/web export paths
need different expectations.

**Change:** use inline progress for the task in progress, stable previews for
media, and a concise operation-specific completion/error message. Do not use
one global spinner or a toast for every autosave. **Acceptance:** the user can
distinguish a queued generation, loading image, saving draft, unavailable host,
and failed download; retrying affects only the failed operation.

### UX-12 / P2: Make Workspace Navigation Explicit

**Evidence:** Sidebar has Images and Notes, a New Chat/New Flow command, and a
logo that returns to chat home (`Sidebar.tsx:165`). In Images, returning to chat
is less explicit than switching into Images; the primary creation label changes
with the current workspace.

**Change:** retain top-positioned creation actions, but give Chat an explicit
workspace destination alongside Images/Notes. Distinguish workspace navigation
from creation commands. Consider New image conversation instead of Flow in
user-facing vocabulary. **Acceptance:** moving between all three workspaces is
obvious without knowing the logo is navigation; narrow labels remain visible.

### UX-13 / P2: History Needs Retrieval, Not Just a Long List

**Evidence:** chat and image histories list recent titles with row menus, but
neither sidebar implementation provides a search field (`Sidebar.tsx:193`,
`images/ImageFlowHistory.tsx:120`). Image flows use generic image icons.

**Change:** add a compact history search entry and optional recency grouping.
Use small result previews for image flows when cheap and already cached.
**Acceptance:** users can find a known old conversation without scrolling
through every recent item; search respects server/device scope and does not
accidentally expose hidden or other-account content.

### UX-14 / P2: Chat Message Toolbars Compete With Reading

**Evidence:** assistant messages can permanently expose Regenerate, Continue,
Copy, Edit, Delete, and Stats (`MessageBubble.tsx:519`), on top of version and
thinking controls. Several adjacent glyphs are not self-explanatory on touch.

**Change:** keep Copy, Regenerate, and a contextual More entry directly
reachable; place occasional/destructive actions in a labeled menu. Keep the
version counter/arrows. Stats must remain available during another generation.
**Acceptance:** no capabilities disappear, actions are usable without hover or
long-press knowledge, and a long conversation has less repeated green chrome.

### UX-15 / P2: Simplify the Chat Composer's Secondary Controls

**Evidence:** attachment, tools, thinking mode, badges, selected notes,
generation notices, and expand state share the composer (`Composer.tsx:434`).
Tool toggles are useful; their bright fills can compete with Send.

**Change:** maintain Attach and Send/Stop as stable anchors. Present tools and
thinking as quieter state controls, with their current state still inspectable.
Keep note attachments visible and removable. **Acceptance:** small widths and
keyboard-open states retain the draft, Send/Stop, expand/collapse, and selected
attachments without overlapping controls or hiding the conversation entirely.

### UX-16 / P1 Validation: Protect Message-Swipe and Height Improvements

**Evidence:** current carousel deliberately renders the active card in normal
flow and adjacent cards on a visible shelf (`MessageVersionCarousel.tsx:27`,
`styles.css:4234`). This addresses prior height/preview issues; it is not a reason
to return to fixed-height clipping or nested message scrollbars.

**Change:** treat this as a regression/performance gate before redesigning more
gestures. **Acceptance:** expand thinking and stats on short/long versions;
swipe repeatedly while another message streams; verify no avatar flicker,
truncated content, lost vertical position, or forced wait between interactions.
Do not report this as currently broken without reproducing it on the device.
The live follow-up confirmed ordinary thinking expansion increases the main
scroll height correctly. It did not exercise carousel transitions or streaming.

### UX-17 / P2: Make Tree Orientation and Selection Clearer

**Evidence:** tree uses preview cards, connectors, edit labels, subtree counts,
fit/zoom controls, and focused inspection (`MessageTreeExplorer.tsx:383`). It
correctly keeps the graph toolbar while a message is focused.

**Change:** retain that structure. Add a clear current-branch marker and a
Find current message command; strengthen the distinction between inspecting a
node and selecting its branch. **Acceptance:** Back from a focused message
returns to the same pan/zoom state; Open This Branch has one predictable effect;
collapsed descendants are represented locally, not by unexplained remote nodes.

### UX-18 / P2: Model Selection Needs Better Recovery and Less Metadata Noise

**Evidence:** picker has useful search, host grouping, and favorites, but its
trigger is disabled when no models exist (`ModelPicker.tsx:393`). Custom options
include several metadata/capability rows; keyboard Escape handling is on the
search field rather than a shared menu lifecycle.

**Change:** keep search/favorites and meaningful host identity. Show full model
details on demand, improve keyboard navigation, and offer actionable empty/error
states instead of only a disabled chooser. **Acceptance:** full names remain
discoverable on mobile, unavailable is distinct from no matches, and an admin
can reach connection repair without hunting elsewhere in settings.

### UX-19 / P2: Reinforce Model-Setting Scope at the Point of Change

**Evidence:** account Models, admin Backends, custom-model versions, and
conversation inference overrides are separate controls. Base model help already
explains its conversation-only effect (`ModelSettingsMenu.tsx:1130`).

**Change:** keep that separation. Use a compact Conversation only scope cue in
the conversation settings header and distinguish Reset this override from
Create custom model. Use consistent personal/server-default labels for images.
**Acceptance:** changing one chat never appears to edit the shared custom model;
creating a reusable model is an explicit action, not an ambiguous Save.

### UX-20 / P2: Finish the Notes Editor's Reduction in Chrome

**Evidence:** full-page editing and the page-like content surface already exist.
The mobile actions are already consolidated into More. A title row, Edit/Preview
row, and formatting row still precede the note (`notes/NotesWorkspace.tsx:944`,
`styles.css:1309`, `styles.css:1445`).

**Change:** preserve the full-page editor and return-to-chat behavior. Combine
mode selection more compactly with the editor toolbar, and avoid showing editing
tools when reading. Keep history/details accessible. **Acceptance:** the note
starts near the top, long titles fit, keyboard focus is obvious despite the
borderless writing surface, and autosave/conflict recovery remains visible.

### UX-21 / P2: Explain Effective Access Without Flattening Permissions

**Evidence:** note access depends on account settings, per-note scope, and chat
tool state. Existing Notes settings already separate actions from defaults for
future notes (`settingsNotes.tsx:256`). Memories have similar layered access.

**Change:** add a compact effective-access summary in note/memory details, with
a link to the specific limiting setting. Keep advanced controls disclosed.
**Acceptance:** a user can determine why the current model cannot see one note
without visiting three unrelated screens. This must describe existing
permissions, not bypass them or change their defaults.

### UX-22 / P2: Separate Memory Content From Memory Configuration

**Evidence:** Memories places global access, past-chat access, and the memory
library on the same settings screen (`settingsMemories.tsx:508`). Ordinary
reviewing/editing is underneath configuration.

**Change:** lead with the memory library and put configuration behind its own
settings action. Retain recoverable Forgotten and history. Keep Notes, Memories,
and Context distinct; they solve different tasks. **Acceptance:** inspecting or
correcting a memory does not require scrolling past permission setup every time.

### UX-23 / P2: Settings Organization Has Outgrown Profile

**Evidence:** Profile now contains identity, theme, native servers, native
updates, PWA installation, and Android download. Mobile settings are a horizontal
strip of up to nine sections (`settingsProfile.tsx:195`, `settingsPage.tsx:95`,
`styles.css:8557`). Some sections are offscreen without a strong overview.

**Change:** split Account from Appearance and This device when doing this pass;
retain a clear admin group. Consider a mobile settings index rather than a
long horizontal strip, with an explicit return path. **Acceptance:** users can
find device update channel, server update channel, theme, and account settings
without guessing; existing deep links continue to work.

### UX-24 / P2: Backend Administration Still Speaks Only Ollama

**Evidence:** Add Backend says Ollama Backend and detection/no-results messages
refer only to Ollama (`settingsBackends.tsx:145`, `settingsBackends.tsx:248`),
although the integration detects Kuno capabilities. Multiple refresh/scan/add
commands are permanently exposed in the section header.

**Change:** use capability-based host summaries: Chat, Images, Embeddings, and
their actual readiness. Clarify that localhost/network discovery is performed
from the Vashti server, not the phone. Consolidate discovery commands without
hiding the normal Add action. **Acceptance:** a Kuno host is understandable
without an API key workaround or conflicting Ollama-only wording; an offline
image capability is not mistaken for a broken account.

### UX-25 / P1 Accessibility: Crop Must Not Require Precise Pointer Dragging

**Evidence:** crop selection is a pointer-driven `role="application"` div;
corner handles are hidden from accessibility and there are no keyboard movement
handlers there (`PersonaAvatarField.tsx:249`).

**Change:** add a keyboard/assistive path for position and crop size, preserve
corner dragging, and remove application semantics unless justified by a full
keyboard interaction model. **Acceptance:** choosing, resizing, positioning, and
resetting a crop can be completed without a mouse or precise multi-touch drag.

### UX-26 / P2: Make Save/Default Boundaries Consistent

**Evidence:** settings forms have save/revert/navigation guards; Notes autosaves
with conflict recovery. Image preferences can close and discard a pending draft,
and mix Save preferences with a separate Save server settings command
(`images/ImagesWorkspace.tsx:310`). Thread controls also expose save/reset/new-flow
default commands (`images/ImageFlow.tsx:1351`).

**Change:** keep thread working settings, account defaults, and server policy in
distinct groups with explicit scope. Guard dirty preference dismissal; give
Save as my defaults and New flow with these settings clear, separate commands.
**Acceptance:** reopening confirms exactly what persisted; closing a sheet does
not silently discard edits that another settings screen would protect.

### UX-27 / P2: Keep Setup, Connections, and Updates Task-Oriented

**Evidence:** setup intentionally accumulates only unchosen settings
(`UserSetupScreen.tsx:17`). Native connection setup, saved servers, and app
updates exist, with a separate server channel (`nativeConnections.tsx:331`,
`nativeUpdates.tsx:129`, `settingsApp.tsx:377`). These distinctions are useful.

**Change:** preserve pending-choice setup rather than replaying onboarding every
release. Make new choices and their data scope easy to scan. In native settings,
show the active server and distinguish App update from Server update in labels
and notifications. **Acceptance:** offline/invalid-address/auth failures have a
recoverable next step; an update does not discard a draft or imply that changing
the app channel also changes the server.

### UX-28 / P2: Calm the Visual System Without Losing Identity

**Evidence:** generic buttons inherit bright fills/glow (`styles.css:182`),
while many borders and scrollbars use the accent. Image controls inherit that
emphasis unless explicitly overridden. Existing background preview already has
separate image/message dimming and portrait/landscape controls.

**Change:** define shared primary, secondary, quiet, selected, and destructive
variants instead of per-screen exceptions. Use neutral rails/dividers and
reserve saturated accents for actions/state. Retain the background preview and
test busy/light/dark images rather than removing customization.
**Acceptance:** one obvious primary action per task, no color-only state cues,
readable disabled/help/emphasis text, visible keyboard focus, and no accidental
loss of contrast across both themes and custom backgrounds.

### UX-29 / P2: Tool Results Should Lead to the Work They Changed

**Evidence:** tool cards provide a summary and expanded raw Arguments/Result;
calculator results have a useful compact presentation (`messageContent.tsx:87`).
Notes already have direct single/multiple reference links
(`notes/MessageNoteReferences.tsx:4`), a good pattern to preserve. Other knowledge
tool outcomes do not get that same navigation in this shared card.

**Change:** extend the structured outcome pattern to supported memory and
past-chat results, with links back to the source record and a return path.
Offer history/restore for mutations through the existing authorized record UI;
do not add a blind generic Undo that could overwrite later edits. Keep JSON
under technical details. **Acceptance:** users can inspect what a tool actually
read or changed without relying on the model's prose or reading raw JSON;
failed/denied/empty results are visibly different from successful mutations.

### UX-30 / P1: Mobile Model Menu Overflows and Moves the App

**Evidence:** independently reproduced in the signed-in extension browser and
in-app browser at 390 x 844. Opening the chat model menu made its right edge
x=401, outside the 390-pixel viewport. Automatic search focus scrolled
`.main-pane` horizontally by 11 pixels, moving the header to x=-11 and clipping
the hamburger. The page root itself did not overflow horizontally.
`styles.css:1870` anchors the menu to the picker; `styles.css:8494` limits its
width relative to the viewport without accounting for the picker's offset.

**Change:** use viewport-clamped placement or a mobile sheet, with search focus
that cannot scroll a hidden-overflow app container sideways. Do not merely hide
the overhanging pixels. **Acceptance:** open/search/close at 320, 390, and
landscape widths; every control stays inside the viewport and ancestor scroll
offsets do not change. Repeat for the nested conversation base-model picker.

### UX-31 / P1: Offer Models Appropriate to the Task

**Evidence:** the live new-custom-model form defaulted to `embeddinggemma:latest`;
the chat model picker also listed it with an Embedding badge. Creation was not
submitted and no inference was attempted. `settingsPersonas.tsx:195` chooses
`firstModelValue`, whose implementation (`settingsModelHelpers.ts:10`) selects
the first model in the first nonempty host, without checking its capability.

**Change:** filter/disable known-ineligible models for the operation. Chat,
custom-model bases, and conversation base overrides need a chat-capable model;
embedding configuration needs an embedding model; image selection needs the
matching advertised image workflow. Prefer an eligible current/default model
over arbitrary list order. Preserve a considered fallback for legacy hosts
with unknown capabilities rather than treating unknown as definitively invalid.
**Acceptance:** an embedding-only model is never the automatic conversational
base; known-ineligible choices explain why they are unavailable; a host with
only embeddings produces an actionable no-chat-model state. Add automated
coverage for mixed/embedding-only/unknown-capability catalogs and every picker
consumer, without requiring a real generation to discover the mismatch.

  Notes links, exact historical note snapshots, and permissions are unchanged.

  conversation tool controls remain a separate restriction.
